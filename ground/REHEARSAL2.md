## Sustained traffic and real processes

`traffic-verification.json` records **three simulated hours** at SF7, SF10 and SF12, four-second heartbeat requests and a new suspicion every 55 seconds. Rolling-hour airtime integrates transmission intervals, including partial packets. SF7 peaks at **25.66656 s/hour**, SF10/SF12 at **32.07168 s/hour**, all below 36 s. Maximum contact gaps are **12 / 61 / 241 s**, respectively. Excess long-range alert rates queue rather than violating duty limits; repeats and heartbeats yield. Additional tests cover quiet heartbeats after a burst, legacy full/partial ledgers, slow requested heartbeat, CRC-protected schedule/reason fields, and restart pacing.

Version-2 frames are 37–42 bytes, with a next-contact window and a reason bit distinguishing duty pacing from a deliberately slower heartbeat. Ground reads version-1 journals. It reports calculated waiting within the advertised window, then stale/lost after a bounded deadline. A configured ten-second stale minimum cannot override the physical/legal long-range interval.

Separate station/ground/placement processes ran on TCP **18091 / 18092 / 18787**, UDP **19901**. The SF7 recorded-footage station continued past **315 seconds** with **27 packets**, **zero duty refusals**, zero queue drops and **33.70 s** calculated budget remaining. Ground received smoke and separate simulated flame events and stored an exact-UUID evidence copy. With the station stopped, ground reached stale at about **20.8 s** receipt age and lost at **30.9 s**, using its announced window and configured grace. Saved JPEG decoded at 1280×720, WebM at 640×360. All started services and preview tabs were stopped/closed and the ports checked clear.

Final suites: **165 station Python**, **16 ground Python**, **83 placement/tools Python**, **27 station JS**, **2 ground JS**, **150 firmware host checks**. The existing upstream Starlette/httpx deprecation warning remains visible. `git diff --check` and JS syntax checks pass.
