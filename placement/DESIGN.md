# ACORN interface design

The planner answers “which launch point sees more forest?” The station answers “what do the cameras show, and which suspicion needs a decision?” Give each answer a single home.

## Visual system

Dark mineral surfaces: canvas #090e13, panel #0f161d, grouped controls #151e27, selected surface #1c2733. Primary text #e3e9ee, secondary #a7b4bf, quiet text #8697a5. Soft blue #8fc3e3 is the sole interaction accent, used for focus and links. Main actions are neutral. Amber #f2a93b means suspected smoke; red #f0594e means unavailable or failed. The local flame sensor is labelled explicitly; its physical LED colours are reported as data, not adopted as interface decoration.

IBM Plex Sans, locally vendored under the SIL Open Font License (static/fonts/OFL-IBM-Plex-Sans.txt and OFL-IBM-Plex-Mono.txt). Proportional labels in sentence case; 12–14 px supporting text, 15–20 px headings, 32 px result figures, 48 px Overview headline. IBM Plex Mono is reserved for coordinates, identifiers and dense numeric tables. Tabular figures throughout. No clock in the planner, tracked capitals or ornamental engine indicators. Small simulation labels stay attached to headline figures and the verdict so isolated screenshots retain their meaning.

## Planner

At 1440 px, map width is 1020 px and the only panel is 420 px. Compare owns the two site figures, one verdict, selected site's prominent balloon height/detail control and three per-camera forest bars. The active site has a 2 px accent rule and its marker is white; height controls explicitly say “Editing A/B · site”. A single simulated label belongs to the comparison heading. Position, shared tilt/VFOV, range and smoke elevation live in a disclosure. No heading, camera activation, pair or reference controls remain. Breakdown owns accounting and scenario/camera tables; Data owns concise provenance and attribution. During recalculation, old results are explicitly marked as previous settings. English is forced in the shared loader; stored preferences and URL parameters cannot change it.

The map is the signature: quiet local terrain beneath bright-mint (#5aefb0) visibility cells and three labelled annular camera sectors. The triangular housing is modelled as adjoining 120° azimuth sectors by product decision, at 0°/120°/240°; half-open seams assign targets once. Tilt/VFOV are applied in each ray's radial vertical plane, so heading only rotates camera ownership and does not change total coverage. The original terrain/canopy LOS and curvature calculation are retained. The annular guides use vertical limits on a level target plane through the launch cell (ground or local canopy+smoke), including curvature; the actual raster uses spatially varying heights. Draw both the inner blind boundary and the outer vertical/range limit. No disconnected grey wedge or overlap legend remains. Grey outside-FOV cells represent vertical exclusion.

Keep the same tokens, IBM Plex and map/panel proportions. The basemap is reduced to 35% saturation, 85% contrast, 32% brightness and 60% display opacity, with 0.6 px blur; the unchanged visibility cells remain the subject. A bounded local canvas cache remaps visible forest to bright mint and lowers water opacity; the legend shares the display tokens. The basemap retains original land-cover hues beneath its muted filter. An opaque rectangle derived from the raster alpha mask bounds navigation; quarter-step zoom lets both default rings fit fully. Controls stay in one corner. The selected site's 1.5 px ring and camera seams are white. The other site has one continuous muted perimeter without seams or a second raster. Secondary place labels yield to camera/site labels when they collide. The legend stays in its own measured bottom strip, using “Vertical blind area” for vertical exclusion and omitting empty occlusion keys. On phones the map has at least 400 px of canvas, the two navigation links remain reachable, and camera/place labels below 480 px yield to the A/B markers. The comparison precedes controls; independent scrolling remains desktop only. Breakdown uses three columns, 44 px rows, units in labels and a closed More metrics disclosure. Shared measured zeros are omitted; unknown values stay visible.

The English-only Overview opens with the real six-part CAD in a generous asymmetric stage. Matte moulded plastic, subtle roughness, glass-like inset lens surfaces, baked vertex AO and a small procedural overcast environment make it read as a physical object. Thin rope geometry suspends it against a quiet blue-grey dawn gradient. Projected callouts and a fixed part-detail slot remain an annotation layer; there are no silhouette outlines. Camera 1/2/3 follow the CAD pod normals, approximately 120° apart. A single toggle shows the owner's continuous 360° observation ring, with direct camera selection and an honest assembly separation. A brief settle and at most five seconds of idle yaw stop on first input; reduced motion is immediate. Vertical touch and ordinary wheel gestures scroll through the stage.

The next centrepiece is an interactive LoRa data-link demo: status reports arrive at the centre and a visitor can make a chosen camera spot smoke. The actual 40/42-byte packet layouts and CRC feed a live DOM/SVG Ground compass and report list. Evidence is collected through a separate Wi-Fi action. A plain CSS phone then shows the camera view, evidence and human decision, using genuine app pixels and a small video. The map section uses responsive WebP captures of the live 360° launch map, with desktop and phone sources, intrinsic dimensions and lazy loading. The section keeps its existing frame and layout. Its figures are described as simulated visible forest. A short closing and licence/source details finish the page; the Overview has no limits band or uncontextualized Mac benchmark figures. Removed and borderline claims are recorded for the owner outside the repository. All runtime assets are local; the renderer sleeps at rest, off-screen and while hidden. See static/vendor/README.md for reproducible assets and verification.

## Station

At desktop width, two larger complete feed frames stack beside their measurement metadata, with a separately scrolling 400 px review queue. The newest three records and oldest unanswered remain visible; older records expand behind their actual count. Chronological jump controls and distinct newest/oldest labels help triage. An open or focused row stays visible through polling and collapse. The pending total appears only in the queue heading. A single event opens initially; other rows show camera and time. Expanding another event is explicit and never restarts or replaces a playing clip during polling. Decisions move into history; correction remains available. Only actual data drives timing, device identity and sensor/LED state. Flame sensing is a separate local-module status, never confirmation of a camera event. Runtime requires only local fonts, CSS, JS, imagery and JSON, without animation or heavy libraries.

## Shared product shell and Ground

All three applications serve the same token stylesheet and local IBM Plex weights (SIL OFL). The 48 px desktop shell uses the compact supplied wordmark, a proportional module label, a quiet connection or navigation context, and the same 32 px controls. Phone headers wrap to preserve readable status and touch targets. Applications stay independently runnable; copy tokens to all three static directories together.

Ground gives the azimuth compass the calm canvas and the received-alert queue a 400 px panel, matching the placement division of attention. Five-degree ticks, cardinal directions and restrained camera sectors support the selected approximate bearing. The short white arrow is explicitly labelled as configured module heading. There are no distance rings or claims of measured position. The selected alert stays first as new packets arrive, keeping its compass and evidence context steady. Alert status has one chip; packet IDs and box dimensions sit behind a disclosure. When the Wi-Fi demo is enabled on desktop, the selected alert's collected clip appears beside the compass, with the snapshot and live feeds available on demand. Live frame requests run only while their disclosure is open. Media is always labelled Wi-Fi-only; the packet link carries neither imagery nor an analysis profile.

Station retains large complete frames, with effective profile parameters next to the measurements, a mounted inactive-camera tile and a static swatch of the LED state actually returned by the API. Simulated MCU data carries an explicit badge. Labelled event disclosures replace glyphs. Polling preserves queue targets while pointer or focus is inside; leaving applies deferred arrivals and opens the next pending record after a decision. No animated LED, fabricated telemetry or profile switch is added.

## Placement language

Overview and the launch map are English only. There is no language control; i18n.js forces English and overwrites an old stored Turkish preference. The legacy translation dictionary is retained but never loaded. Ground and Station remain independent applications.
